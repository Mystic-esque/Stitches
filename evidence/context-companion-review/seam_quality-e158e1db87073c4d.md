# Seam Puckering Diagnosis & Scrap Testing

Seam puckering observation, scrap-test procedure, stitch-release diagnostic, tension-adjustment remedy, conditional sample-only comparison, issue patterns for unresolved puckering, and applicability to Sandy and Tina candidates

## Observation

Seam puckering presents as ripples along a newly sewn seam. Before altering a finished garment, test whether thread tension may be contributing using a scrap-fabric sample with matching material and machine settings.

## Stage 1: Does a test seam relax when the stitches are released? (companion-test-scrap-stitch-release-20261001)

Sew a short sample on scrap fabric only, using the same machine, thread, and settings as the puckered seam. Carefully release both threads along a small section (a few centimetres) without pulling or damaging the fabric. Compare the released and still-stitched sections.

**Three possible outcomes:**

### Not-tested
No sample test has been performed. Proceed to perform the scrap-stitch-release test first. Do not advance to a tension-adjustment comparison without this result.

### Unchanged
The ripples remain flat after stitches are removed, or the sample ripples persist when stitches are released. Cause remains unresolved (companion-issue-unresolved-pucker-20261001). Do not recommend a machine-adjustment fix. Other causes include material structure, feeding, needle choice, or thread choice. Do not remove stitches from the actual garment based on this result alone.

### Relaxes
The ripples flatten when stitches are removed. Thread tension may be contributing (companion-issue-tension-pucker-20261001). This result permits moving to the optional separate comparison step below. A relaxed sample suggests tension but does not establish it as the only cause.

## Stage 2: Compare a lower-tension sample (companion-remedy-sample-lower-tension-20261001)

**Eligibility:** Only proceed if the stitch-release test outcome is "relaxes." If the outcome is "not-tested" or "unchanged," this step is not eligible; return to the diagnostic test or seek another cause.

On another scrap seam only, try a modestly lower upper-thread tension while maintaining a balanced stitch. Change one setting at a time and sew a new test sample. Compare the new sample with the original.

**Confirmation check:** Does the new sample lie flatter while both sides still form a balanced, secure stitch? 

- **Yes:** the tension adjustment may reduce puckering. Further testing on the actual garment is a separate decision.
- **No or unbalanced:** Stop this path and seek another cause. Do not apply this tension adjustment.

## Applicability

Applies to Sandy circle skirt and Tina faux-wrap top planning candidates.
