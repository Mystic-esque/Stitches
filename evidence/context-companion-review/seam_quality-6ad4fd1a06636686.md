# Seam Puckering Diagnosis & Scrap Testing

Seam puckering observation, scrap-test procedure, stitch-release diagnostic, tension-adjustment remedy, conditional sample-only comparison, issue patterns for unresolved puckering, and applicability to Sandy and Tina candidates

## Overview

Seam puckering appears as ripples along a sewn seam. Whether thread tension is a contributing cause depends on a controlled test: does the puckering relax when stitches are carefully released from a matching scrap sample? The answer determines next steps, or whether the cause remains unresolved.

All procedures here apply to both Sandy circle skirt and Tina faux-wrap top candidates. Work on scrap fabric only; never unpick or alter the finished garment based on these tests.

**Source: Coats technical bulletin: Eliminating Seam Puckering** (companion-source-coats-pucker-20261001), pending review.

## Diagnostic pathway

### Step 1: Perform the stitch-release test

Use scrap material matching your project fabric. Sew a short sample seam with the same machine, thread, and settings you used on the puckered seam [1].

Carefully release (unpick) both threads along a small section of the sample—roughly 2–3 cm—without pulling or damaging the fabric. Do not do this on the actual garment [1].

Compare the released section with the section still holding stitches. Outcome rules [1]:

- **Relaxes:** The ripples flatten when stitches come out. Proceed to Step 2.
- **Unchanged:** The ripples remain even after stitch removal. Cause remains unresolved (companion-issue-unresolved-pucker-20261001). Thread tension is not the issue. Other causes include material structure, feeding, and needle/thread choice [2]. Do not recommend a machine-adjustment change without another diagnostic check.
- **Not-tested:** You have not yet performed this test. Perform it before proceeding.

**Test ID: Does a test seam relax when the stitches are released?** (companion-test-scrap-stitch-release-20261001) [1].

### Step 2: Compare a lower-tension sample (if test result is "relaxes" only)

This step is eligible only if the stitch-release test showed the ripples relaxed. Do not attempt this comparison if the result was "unchanged" or "not-tested."

On another piece of scrap fabric, sew a new seam with the same setup as before [3]. This time, lower the upper-thread tension modestly while maintaining a balanced stitch—the underside should still show secure, even stitches [3].

**Confirmation check:** Does the new sample lie flatter than the original, while both sides still form a balanced, secure stitch? If not, stop this comparison path and seek another cause [3].

**Comparison ID: Compare a lower-tension sample** (companion-remedy-sample-lower-tension-20261001) [3].

**Important:** This is a test-sample experiment on scrap fabric. It does not authorize you to alter, unpick, or restitch the finished garment [3].
