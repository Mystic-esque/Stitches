# Seam Puckering Diagnosis & Scrap Testing

Seam-puckering observation, thread-tension diagnostics, scrap-fabric sample testing procedure, conditional sample-only comparison, remedy steps, and applicability to Sandy and Tina garments

Seam puckering—ripples along a sewn seam—can result from thread tension, material structure, needle choice, or thread feeding. Before unpicking or altering a garment, test the cause on scrap fabric to avoid wasting work or fabric.

## Observable symptom

Ripples appear along a newly sewn test seam and relax when stitches are removed from a short sample section. This pattern suggests thread tension may be contributing [1], though it does not establish tension as the only cause [1].

## Diagnostic test: Scrap-stitch release

Use matching scrap material only [2]. Sew a short sample seam with the same machine, thread, and settings as your garment [2]. Carefully release (unthread) both threads along a small section—just a few centimetres—without pulling or damaging the fabric [2]. Compare the released section to the still-stitched section [2].

**Maker question:** Do the ripples flatten when you carefully remove a few centimetres of stitches?

**Possible outcomes:**
- **Relaxes:** Ripples flatten. Thread tension may be contributing; proceed to sample-tension test below [1].
- **Unchanged:** Ripples persist after stitch release. Cause remains unresolved; other factors (material structure, needle choice, thread feed) may be involved [3]. Do not alter machine settings without another check [3].
- **Not tested:** No controlled sample has been checked. Proceed to sample-tension test if you have observed puckering in your initial seam [3].

**Safety notes:** Do not remove stitches from the actual garment for this preliminary test; stop if the sample fabric is being damaged [2]. A relaxed sample suggests tension but does not establish it as the only cause; do not unpick a finished garment from this result alone [1].

## Remedy: Compare a lower-tension sample

If the scrap-stitch release test shows relaxation, test a modestly lower upper-thread tension on another scrap seam [4]. Change one setting at a time and compare it with the original sample [4].

**Confirmation:** Does the new sample lie flatter while both sides still form a balanced, secure stitch? If not, stop this path and seek another cause [4].

**Safety notes:** This is a test-sample experiment only, not authorization to alter, unpick, or restitch the finished garment [4]. Do not remove stitches from the actual garment on the strength of this test [4].

## Applicability

Seam-puckering observation and diagnostic testing apply to the Sandy circle skirt and Tina faux-wrap top planning candidates [1][2]. All testing must use scrap material matching the intended garment fabric [2].
