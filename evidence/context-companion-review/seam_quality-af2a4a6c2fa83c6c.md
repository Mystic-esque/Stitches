# Seam Puckering Diagnosis & Scrap Testing

Seam puckering observation, scrap-test procedure, stitch-release diagnostic, tension-adjustment remedy, conditional sample-only comparison, issue patterns for unresolved puckering, and applicability to Sandy and Tina candidates

## Observation and diagnostic pathway

Seam puckering appears as ripples along a newly sewn seam. Before altering a finished garment, use a scrap-fabric sample to test whether thread tension is contributing.

The diagnostic pathway has three stages, determined by a preliminary scrap-stitch-release test:

### Stage 1: Does a test seam relax when the stitches are released?

On scrap material only, sew a short sample using the same machine, thread, and settings as the puckered seam. Carefully release both threads along a small section (a few centimetres) without pulling or damaging the fabric. Compare the released and still-stitched sections.

**Possible outcomes:**

- **Not-tested:** Perform this scrap-stitch-release test first before considering any adjustments.
- **Unchanged:** The ripples remain after stitches are removed. This means the cause remains unresolved (companion-issue-unresolved-pucker-20261001). Do not recommend a machine adjustment; other causes include material structure, feeding, needle choice, or thread choice.
- **Relaxes:** The ripples flatten when stitches are removed. Thread tension may be contributing (companion-issue-tension-pucker-20261001). You are eligible to proceed to Stage 2.

Do not remove stitches from the actual garment for this preliminary test. Stop immediately if the sample fabric is being damaged.

### Stage 2: Compare a lower-tension sample (sample-only, if relaxes outcome)

This stage is eligible only if the Stage 1 result was **relaxes**. On another scrap seam only, try a modestly lower upper-thread tension while maintaining a balanced stitch. Change one setting at a time and compare it with the original sample.

**Confirmation check:** Does the new sample lie flatter while both sides still form a balanced, secure stitch? If not, stop this path and seek another cause.

If the result is not-tested, unchanged, failed, or unbalanced, stop this path. Do not attempt automatic repeated adjustments or assume a confirmed diagnosis.
