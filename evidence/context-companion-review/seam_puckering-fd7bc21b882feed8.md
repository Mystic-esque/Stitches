# Seam Puckering Diagnosis & Scrap Testing

Seam-puckering observation, thread-tension diagnostics, scrap-fabric sample testing procedure, conditional sample-only comparison, remedy steps, and applicability to Sandy and Tina garments

Two issue patterns and one diagnostic test govern seam-puckering work in Stitches. Both apply to the Sandy circle skirt and Tina faux-wrap top planning candidates. All records carry **pending** review status: they support education and planning, not maker-approved instructions.

## Observable symptoms

- **Tension-contributing pattern** (`tension-pucker`): ripples appear along a newly sewn test seam and **relax** when stitches are removed from a short sample section [1].
- **Unresolved-cause pattern** (`unresolved-pucker`): a test seam still ripples after a short stitch section is removed, **or** no controlled sample has been tested yet [2].

## Scrap-stitch-release test (required first step)

Before any machine adjustment, perform this test on **scrap fabric only** — never on the actual garment [4].

1. Cut matching scrap from the same material used for the garment.
2. Sew a short sample seam with the same machine, thread, and settings as the garment seam in question.
3. Carefully release both threads along a small section without pulling or damaging the fabric.
4. Compare the released section with the still-stitched section.

**Possible outcomes and what they mean:**

| Outcome | Interpretation | Next step |
|---|---|---|
| `relaxes` | Tension is likely contributing | Eligible for lower-tension sample comparison |
| `unchanged` | Cause remains unresolved | Do not adjust machine; seek another cause |
| `not-tested` | Test has not been performed | Perform this test first; do not proceed |

Risk note: stop immediately if the sample fabric is being damaged during stitch removal [4].

## Lower-tension sample comparison (conditional)

This comparison is **only eligible** if the scrap-stitch-release test result is `relaxes`. If the result is `unchanged` or `not-tested`, this path is closed — return to the test or investigate another cause [3].

Safe action: on **another scrap seam only**, try a modestly lower upper-thread tension while maintaining a balanced stitch. Change one setting at a time and compare with the original sample [3].

Confirmation check: does the new sample lie flatter while both sides still form a balanced, secure stitch? If not, stop this path and seek another cause [3].

**This is a test-sample experiment only.** It is not authorization to alter, unpick, or restitch the finished garment [3].

## Risk boundaries

- A relaxed sample suggests tension as a contributing factor but does not establish it as the **only** cause; do not unpick a finished garment from this result alone [1].
- Other causes of puckering include material structure, feeding, and needle/thread choice [2].
- Do not recommend a particular machine adjustment without completing the scrap-stitch-release test [2].
