# Seam Puckering Diagnosis & Scrap Testing

Seam puckering observation, scrap-test procedure, stitch-release diagnostic, tension-adjustment remedy, conditional sample-only comparison, issue patterns for unresolved puckering, and applicability to Sandy and Tina candidates

## Observation and scrap testing

Seam puckering appears as ripples along a newly sewn seam. Before altering a finished garment, test whether thread tension may be contributing by working with scrap material matching the garment's fabric [1].

Using the same machine, thread, and settings as the garment, sew a short sample seam on scrap fabric. This is the preparatory step for all subsequent diagnosis [2].

## Diagnostic test: Does a test seam relax when the stitches are released?

The stitch-release test branches the diagnosis into two paths [3]:

**How to perform:** On the short sample seam, carefully release both threads along a small section (a few centimetres) without pulling or damaging the fabric. Compare the released section with the still-stitched section to see whether the ripples flatten [3].

**Possible outcomes** [3]:

- **Relaxes**: The ripples flatten when stitches are removed. This suggests "Thread tension may be contributing" (companion-issue-tension-pucker-20261001) and makes the lower-tension sample comparison eligible [4].
- **Unchanged**: The ripples persist even after stitches are removed. "Cause remains unresolved" (companion-issue-unresolved-pucker-20261001); other factors (material structure, feeding, needle or thread choice) may be at play [5]. Do not proceed to a tension-based remedy without another investigation.
- **Not-tested**: No controlled sample has been tested yet. Perform this scrap-stitch-release test first before considering any adjustment [3].

**Safety notes:** Use scrap material only; do not remove stitches from the actual garment for this preliminary test. Stop if the sample fabric is being damaged [3].

## Tension-based remedy: Compare a lower-tension sample

If the stitch-release test result is **relaxes**, the next step is eligible: "Compare a lower-tension sample" (companion-remedy-sample-lower-tension-20261001) [6].

On another scrap seam, try a modestly lower upper-thread tension while maintaining a balanced stitch. Change one setting at a time and compare the new sample with the original sample [6].

**Confirmation check:** Does the new sample lie flatter while both sides still form a balanced, secure stitch? If not, stop this path and seek another cause [6].

**This is a test-sample experiment only, not authorization to alter, unpick, or restitch the finished garment.** If the stitch-release test result is not-tested or unchanged, this lower-tension comparison is not eligible; return to the diagnostic test or seek another cause [6].
